## Early lawn-mowing business

At approximately eight years old, Codie and his brother bought a petrol-powered lawnmower and created a small lawn-care service in Zimbabwe.

Petrol power was useful because electricity supply was unreliable.

They approached residents at properties managed by their parents’ company, arranged for gardeners to perform the work and earned recurring weekly revenue.

The business funded tuck-shop spending and helped Codie save towards buying a dog.

It ended when boarding school made continued involvement difficult.

This is an early example of:

- identifying a practical problem;
- buying an income-producing asset;
- arranging labour;
- recurring revenue;
- saving towards a goal.

## Zimbabwe property access research

Codie is deeply interested in the lack of conventional property finance in Zimbabwe.

The problem:

- mortgages are difficult to access;
- wages are low relative to property prices;
- many transactions require full cash payment;
- people may save meaningful amounts but remain unable to buy a whole property.

Possible ideas include:

- fractional ownership;
- SPVs;
- shared equity;
- tenant-to-ownership structures;
- property investment clubs;
- development partnerships;
- rental-income vehicles.

This is research, not a live investment product.

Any future structure would require legal, regulatory, tax, governance and investor-protection work.

The website must never imply that investments are currently being offered or guaranteed.
